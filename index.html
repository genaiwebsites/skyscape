About the pilot

Eyes in the sky. Heart on earth.

I'm Kshitiz Bathwal. I fly a small aircraft with a one-inch sensor bolted underneath it, and I point it straight down. What comes back is a country most people have lived in their whole lives and never actually seen — rivers that only draw straight from 400 feet, fields that turn into typography, coastlines that keep redrawing themselves twice a day.

The work is mostly waiting. For light, for wind under 12 knots, for the one twenty-minute window when a dry lake goes copper. Skyscape isn't a studio — it's a habit of leaving before sunrise and coming back with something that argues with the map.

"Every place looks like nowhere until you see it from above — then it looks like everywhere."
